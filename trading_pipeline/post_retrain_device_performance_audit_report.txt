PART 4 -- PANDAS VS. POLARS THREADING CHECK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FINDING:
In both feature_engine/zscore.py and feature_engine/indicators.py, operations are strictly:
(a) Pandas methods (.rolling(), .ewm(), .groupby().transform(), .groupby().apply())
These run single-threaded on exactly one CPU core. When groupby().transform(lambda x: ...) executes, Python's Global Interpreter Lock (GIL) serializes each session's evaluation. On a multi-core processor (e.g. 8 to 16 cores), 87% to 93% of CPU compute capacity sits completely idle during Stage 2.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PART 5 -- CONCLUDING SUMMARY & ARCHITECTURAL VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The performance profile indicates that XGBoost GPU execution is functioning as intended:
Models 1 and 2 train nearly one million samples in ~3.0 seconds each, and Model 3 trains 5.14 million samples in 7.48 seconds on CUDA, achieving 74% to 85% GPU utilization with zero silent CPU fallback.

However, the CPU bottleneck resides in Stage 2 (Feature Engineering), which accounts for 44.10% (14.77 seconds) of total processing time per company. This bottleneck is not caused by disk I/O, CV splitting, or replay buffer management (which together account for under 9% of runtime), but is directly driven by 19 single-threaded Pandas groupby().transform(lambda x: ...) and groupby().apply() calls in zscore.py and indicators.py. Because Pandas executes Python lambdas across intraday session groups sequentially under the Python GIL, CPU cores remain underutilized while a single core runs at 100%.

Migrating zscore.py and indicators.py to native Polars expressions (utilizing expressions such as pl.col(...).rolling_mean(...).over("session") and Rust-level multi-threading) in a future optimization pass could reduce feature engineering time from ~15 seconds to under 1.5 seconds per company. This would reduce per-company cycle time from ~33.5 seconds to ~19.5 seconds, saving over 23 minutes across a full 100-company retrain.
